THE SIMPLE IDEA

The fish leads.
The sake follows.

“Sake for sushi” is too broad to be useful. Sea bream, fatty tuna, salmon, shellfish and a spicy roll each ask for something different from the glass. Match weight, aroma, acidity and umami to the sushi, rather than reaching automatically for the most expensive bottle.

魚の個性を消さず、引き立てる一杯を。

THREE PAIRING PRINCIPLES

A better sushi pairing starts with three simple ideas.

01 重

Match the weight.

Delicate fish wants a lighter, cleaner sake. Richer fish can carry more body, texture and savory depth.

02 酸

Refresh richness.

Salmon and toro bring fat. Acidity and a clean finish can reset the palate between bites.

03 香

Use fruit with heat.

With spicy rolls or salty toppings, gentle fruit can soften the edges without overwhelming the food.

FAQ

Before your next sushi night.

Is Junmai Daiginjo always the best sake for sushi?＋

No. Grade alone does not tell you whether the sake matches the fish, fat, sauce or texture. Taste and balance matter more at the table.

Should sake with sushi always be chilled?＋

Many aromatic styles work well chilled, but richer Junmai, Kimoto and Yamahai styles can also work closer to room temperature or warm, depending on the sushi.

What sake works with fatty tuna?＋

Look for enough body, acidity or savory depth to stand beside the fat. A very light sake can disappear next to toro.

What is an easy starting point for sushi beginners?＋

A clean, lightly fruity style is accessible because it works with many delicate fish and does not require specialist vocabulary.
